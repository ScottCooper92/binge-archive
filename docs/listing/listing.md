# Store listing (English, en-GB)

## App name

**Internet Archive for Binge** (27 of 30 characters)

Why: it names the source first and the player second. A listing named only "Internet Archive"
would read as the Archive's own app, which it is not. The name is a maintainer's decision; see the
README.

## Full description

4,000 characters at most.

> A companion for Binge. Open a film in Binge, and if the Internet Archive holds a copy in the
> public domain or under an open licence, Binge offers it to play.
>
> **Open licences by default.** Out of the box, this app returns a copy only if its licence is the
> public domain, CC0 or CC BY. Each copy names its licence on the row.
>
> **Your choice of sources.** In the app's Sources settings you can turn off title matches, keep to
> the public domain only, accept any Creative Commons licence, set a minimum quality of 720p or
> 1080p, or ask only for copies with subtitles in your language. You can also choose which
> licences to include. Each copy still shows its licence, or says it states none. The settings stay
> on your device.
>
> **Mostly older films.** Most public-domain films are from before 1960, so many titles will have
> nothing here. Series are not covered yet.
>
> **Plays nothing itself.** This app finds the copies; Binge plays them. Without Binge, it shows
> what it does, how fresh its film index is and the films it found for you recently.
>
> **Nothing about you.** No account, no sign-in, no key. The app asks Wikidata and the Internet
> Archive about the film you opened, and nothing else. No analytics, no crash reporting, no adverts.
> The films it found recently and any copies you reported stay on your device, and you can clear
> them.
>
> **Phone and Android TV.** In English and Spanish.
>
> Film data from Wikidata (CC0). Films from the Internet Archive. Neither is affiliated with this
> app.
>
> Open source, under the Apache 2.0 licence: github.com/ScottCooper92/binge-archive.
